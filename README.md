## Purpose

Our project is a teacher review website for our high school, inspired by Rate My Professors. We want to give students a place to share their experiences with teachers and classes so other students have a better idea of what to expect.

A course description can tell you what subjects you'll study, but it usually doesn't explain what a normal day in that class feels like, how much homework you might have, or how the teacher approaches a difficult topic. Those are the kinds of details we want students to be able to share.

The idea comes from the questions students ask each other when choosing classes. How hard is the course? Does the teacher explain things clearly? What should I know before signing up?

Usually, the answers depend on who you happen to know. Someone with older friends might hear several different perspectives, while someone else might have very little information. Our goal is to bring those conversations into one place and make them easier for everyone to access.

We also want the feedback to be useful and fair. Different students can have completely different experiences in the same classroom, so one rating cannot tell the whole story. We hope students will explain what worked for them, what they struggled with, and what advice they would give someone taking the class next year.

Specific comments about assignments, teaching styles, and expectations can help readers decide what matters to them.

We're building this project for Third Space's first week, with the theme **"harvest."** Our interpretation is about gathering what students have learned from their experiences and sharing it with the school community.

We also brought the theme into the design through autumn colors, original pixel art, a walking turkey animation, and custom sound effects. We wanted the site to have some personality and feel like something made by students.

The project is still being developed, but the purpose is straightforward: help students learn from one another and feel more prepared for their classes.

It is also a chance for our team to practice building a website together, combining programming, artwork, animation, audio, and documentation into a project connected to our own school experience.

## Features

Juntos lets students share information about teachers and classes in one place. Students can make posts about their experiences, browse what other students have written, and use the site to get a better idea of what a class might actually be like.

Some of the main features we worked on include:
- creating and viewing posts
- user profiles/accounts
- image uploads
- different pages for browsing and interacting with the site
- a harvest-themed design
- original pixel art (In main, but is primarily in  longfei-images)
- a walking turkey animation (In branch right now)
- custom button, posting, and scrolling sound effects
- background music
- responsive/interactive UI elements

We tried to make the website useful while also giving it its own style instead of making it look like a basic review site.

## How We Made It

We started by deciding what kind of website would fit the harvest theme while also being useful to students at our school. The idea of "harvest" became more than just the visual theme. We thought of students gathering experiences from classes and then sharing that information with other students. From there, we split the project into different parts. We worked on the website layout, backend, database, user interactions, artwork, animations, and sound. The site went through a lot of changes while we worked on it. Instead of designing everything perfectly at the beginning, we kept testing things, changing the layout, and adding features as the project developed. We also used GitHub branches so multiple people could work on different parts of the project without constantly editing the same files.

## Challenges We Ran Into / Problems

One challenge was working on the same project with multiple people. Sometimes changes from one branch affected files that another person had also edited, so we had to learn how to merge changes and fix conflicts. Another challenge was getting all of the frontend and backend parts to work together. A feature might look correct on the page but still need changes to the JavaScript, server, or database before it actually worked. We also had smaller problems with things like file paths and assets. For example, our sound effects had to be placed in the correct static folders and referenced with the correct paths before the browser could load them. Audio was another thing we had to work around because browsers do not always allow background music to autoplay. We made the music start after the user interacts with the page instead.
